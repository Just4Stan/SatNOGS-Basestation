CubeSats offer universities an accessible route to independent space research, but successfully receiving their telemetry requires a ground station that is affordable, portable, and compatible with open ecosystems such as SatNOGS. Commercial antenna rotators cost \euro{}760 to \euro{}1300; the open SatNOGS v3 design still requires \euro{}300--500 in parts. This thesis investigates how far costs can be reduced without compromising pointing accuracy or SatNOGS integration.

This thesis describes the design, implementation, and field validation of a portable, SatNOGS-compatible ground station for the AetherSpace CubeSat at 433\,MHz. The station consists of a Raspberry Pi 3A+ with two subsystems.

The rotator uses a 3D-printed ASA structure with integrated printed bearing races and steel balls, geared DC motors (516:1 internal, up to 1376:1 total), and a custom RP2040 motor controller PCB. A PID loop achieves sub-0.5\degree\ elevation and 0--3\degree\ azimuth error, validated during live passes. Elevation homing uses an ADXL345 accelerometer; azimuth is calibrated via the phone compass or by powering up pre-aligned to north.

The RF front-end is a 6-layer Pi HAT carrying two TI CC1200 transceivers; the UHF (433\,MHz) path is assembled with a matching network derived from the TI CC1200EM reference, while the VHF (144/145\,MHz) path is routed but not populated.

A Python stack on the Pi handles pass prediction, Doppler correction, per-satellite CC1200 reconfiguration, and SiDS telemetry submission. An HTTPS dashboard provides laptop-based field control, with optional phone access. The station speaks standard EasyComm III over hamlib.

Across three field sessions, approximately 210 satellite passes were tracked with a Siretta Oscar 44 Yagi. The CC1200 receive chain was validated for local test frames in short-range ground-to-ground tests but no protocol-valid frames were decoded from orbit. Board-level interference (buck-converter harmonics, RP2040 digital noise, Pi GPIO emissions) raises the effective noise floor approximately 30~dB above the thermal\,+\,NF baseline to $-$96~dBm, well above the received signal of $-$125.7~dBm at 30\degree\ elevation, making orbital decode impossible without an external LNA. The link-budget analysis identifies the absence of a front-end LNA as the dominant cause of the remaining sensitivity gap. The v2 MOTOR\_RF\_HAT (combined controller/RF board) is design-complete but not fabricated at submission.
